# Review 1 — Product & Candidate Experience

Reviewer role: candidate funnel, copy/flow, fairness perception, mobile, RTL/typography UX, edge cases. Not covering backend, scoring math internals, or security.

## CRITICAL

1. **Pay/contractor terms are hidden behind a full personal-details form, not shown on landing.** The landing page (`/jobs/{slug}`) has only a "one-paragraph hook" and a CTA; the structured terms card (85 ₪/h, contractor, Rishon-only) appears in *step 2*, only after the candidate has already filled name, DOB, phone, email, institution, degree, year, and GPA in step 1. A candidate who would self-select out on rate or contractor status only discovers that after handing over real PII — this reads as bait-and-switch even though unintentional, and pollutes the funnel with applicants who bounce at step 2 having already "paid" the effort cost. **Fix:** put a compact terms summary on the landing page itself, before the personal-details form.

2. **The documented resume/re-entry flow depends on email, but email is off by default at launch.** `CANDIDATE_FLOW.md` §2.2 and the `/resume` route rely on OTP-by-email for continuing after a lost cookie, switching devices, or a duplicate application. `OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` #2 sets `EMAIL_ENABLED=false` as the launch default, with re-entry then requiring "an admin-sent resume link" — a fact that appears nowhere in candidate-facing copy. A candidate who clears cookies, switches from phone to laptop, or just comes back a week later on a different browser has no visible path forward and no explanation of what to do. **Fix:** either enable email for candidate-facing transactional flows independent of the admin digest toggle, or add explicit candidate-facing copy for the no-email case (who to contact, what to expect).

3. **No closure or timeframe for candidates after the assessment; non-advancing candidates are apparently never told.** The done page ("נחזור אליך... אם נרצה להמשיך לשלב ראיון") gives no expected response window, and nothing in the pipeline design sends any signal to candidates who are not moving forward — i.e., silent rejection by default for the majority of applicants. For CS students comparing several offers, ~40+ minutes invested (form + 28-minute timed test) with indefinite, possibly permanent silence is a real employer-brand cost and a plausible source of negative word-of-mouth in a small, connected student population. **Fix:** state an expected response window on the done page; consider a low-effort "not moving forward" notice for rejected candidates.

## IMPORTANT

4. **Step 3 stacks rules, an "AI use is detected" line, and a monitoring disclosure back-to-back right before the timer starts, with no confidence-building counterweight.** The design's own pilot target (≥70% of strong students finish each item type with ≥15% time left) is never surfaced to candidates — only the surveillance/rules framing is. This is the single highest-anxiety moment in the funnel; as written it reads more like a warning than a briefing. **Fix:** add one reassuring line grounded in the pilot data, and soften "שימוש בהם מזוהה ומפחית מאמינות התוצאה" to something less accusatory in tone.

5. **The first investigation item (210s, 5–6 tabs, a UI pattern nothing earlier in the test uses) is introduced only via a passive 4-screenshot walkthrough, not an interactive practice.** Candidates will likely spend real timed seconds on their first "חקירה" item just learning how tabs/artifacts work, taxing that item unfairly relative to later ones — and independence is explicitly called out as the pillar the hiring manager cares about most. **Fix:** an untimed, interactive one-artifact practice item before block 3 begins.

6. **The "average doesn't gate" promise is undercut by the admin's own filter capability.** Step 1 tells candidates the GPA field is "נתון עזר בלבד ואינו פוסל מועמדות," but `ADMIN_UX.md` §3.3 lists an average-range slider as an admin filter/sort option on the candidate list. Even if intended as "informational," giving admins an operational filter on a field candidates were told is inert is a trust risk if it's ever exercised — and since it's unverified self-report, any ambiguity about whether it "really" matters increases the incentive to round up. **Fix:** either remove average from the filterable/sortable admin columns to make the promise literally true, or make the "not used to rank" caveat much more prominent to candidates.

7. **Step 2's Rishon confirmation doesn't acknowledge a "לא" answer given one screen earlier.** A candidate who answered "לא" to "יכולת לעבוד מראשון לציון" in step 1 is then asked in step 2 to check an unconditional box: "הבנתי שהעבודה דורשת יכולת להגיע פיזית לאזור ראשון לציון." Nothing reminds them this is still non-blocking, which can read as contradictory ("didn't I just say I can't — am I being disqualified?"). **Fix:** branch the copy or add a line referencing their earlier answer when it was "לא."

8. **No described failure/progress UX for the optional CV upload, unlike the assessment's documented retry UX.** Step 1's error handling section only covers assessment-answer submission (§5, §8 of `CANDIDATE_FLOW.md`); nothing addresses a stalled or failed upload of a near-5MB file on a slow mobile connection during the step-1 submit, or whether a CV failure can block/lose the rest of the (already-typed) personal details. **Fix:** make CV upload asynchronous and non-blocking relative to the rest of the form, with its own progress indicator and an explicit "continue without CV" recovery path.

9. **Mobile candidates hit the desktop-only gate late, after the most tedious step.** Only the assessment enforces ≥900px; steps 1–2 have no stated device expectation, and mobile is realistically where a lot of first-touch application traffic originates (shared links, WhatsApp, social). A candidate who fills the full personal-details form and reads the job description on a phone only learns they need a computer once they reach the step 3 device check. **Fix:** flag the "you'll need a computer for the timed test" expectation on the landing page or at the top of step 1, not first at the device-check gate.

## MINOR

10. The "מה מקבלים" section of the job description ("אחריות משמעותית, חשיפה טכנולוגית רחבה מאוד...") leans generic/corporate next to the otherwise blunt, specific tone elsewhere in the same description ("זו לא משרת Help Desk"). Worth tightening to match the sharper voice used for the tech-ops disclosure.

11. Bidi reading overhead (Hebrew question text wrapping LTR JSON/log/code snippets) is a real extra scanning cost, most consequential in the 20-second speed block. `TEST_STRATEGY.md` §9's pilot should explicitly check whether the per-item time budgets already absorb this, rather than assuming pilot testers' fluency generalizes to the full applicant pool.

12. Nothing in step 1 reassures candidates that partial progress isn't at risk if they're interrupted mid-form (a phone call, an accidental tab close) before submitting — since the step is one transaction, an interruption loses everything typed so far. A one-line "מילוי הטופס לוקח כ-3 דקות" type expectation-setter would reduce abandonment anxiety.

13. The "already completed" duplicate-application message ("כבר השלמת את התהליך למשרה זו") is a dead end with no next step or link offered — a small but avoidable closure gap, same family of issue as Critical #3.

## Verdict

The core flow is well thought through and several of the hardest product tensions — honesty about the tech-ops/support component, non-blocking Rishon and GPA fields, server-authoritative timing explained up front — are handled more carefully than most screening tools bother to. The real risk is not any single broken step but a pattern of **sequencing and closure gaps**: terms are revealed after PII is collected instead of before, the recovery path for a lost session quietly depends on a feature that's off by default at launch, and the candidate experience ends in silence for everyone who isn't advanced. None of this requires redesigning the assessment or the scoring — it's copy, ordering, and a couple of default-config choices — but left as-is it will cost completion rate at the top of the funnel and goodwill at the bottom, in a small, well-networked applicant pool where both are expensive to lose.
